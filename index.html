    From orbit

    Start here. End at our gate.

    That fist of land pushing into the Arabian Sea is Saurashtra. Our whole working world is a strip of its southern shore that you could cover with a thumbnail from up here.

    Scroll, and come down.

    Gujarat

    The old name is Kathiawar.

    Lions in the middle. Salt at the edges. And along the south coast a seam of white limestone that the cement makers found in the early 1980s and have not left since.

    Rajula and Jafrabad talukas, Amreli

    Forty kilometres of shore.

    Between Jafrabad in the west and Chanch in the east: two cement works, a container port, a shipyard, gas terminals, salt pans and an island village. Inside this frame our crews answer a call in under thirty minutes.

    Why distance decides an emergency

    Real elevation

    Now the ground has height.

    From here the land stands on real elevation data, stretched about four times so that a flat coast can be read from the air. White is limestone. The sea is brown with the silt of the Gulf of Khambhat, stirred by some of the highest tides in India.

    20.866° N, 71.366° E

    Jafrabad

    A creek packed with fishing boats. Bombay duck drying on the racks. Salt pans behind the town, and a cement works on the shore since 1982.

    Walk through Jafrabad

    The Bhakodar and Kovaya pits

    The white pits

    Limestone, cut in benches four to eight metres high and hauled to the kilns. Keep scrolling. The picture changes to the same ground every March since 2017. The pits widen, and the worked-out ones fill with green water.

    Read the pits

    20.9065° N, 71.4535° E

    Kovaya

    A village of about four thousand people with one of India's big cement works at its door. In 2015 one excavator and a crew of six started work from here. This is where the phone is answered.

    Kovaya, our home and how we began

    The cement jetty

    Three kilometres into the sea

    The works do not need a road to reach a ship. This jetty carries cement and clinker about 3.4 kilometres out to deep water. We measured it on this image.

    What a cement works asks of us

    The gas port

    The curve that was not there

    In March 2017 this was open sea. Keep scrolling, and watch a breakwater grow out of the water and curl around a new port for liquefied gas. Somebody tipped that rock, one truck at a time.

    Energy on this coast

    20.922° N, 71.508° E

    Port Pipavav

    India's first private port. Cargo since 1996. Containers, cars, bulk and liquids, and since 2006 trains that carry containers two high.

    Pipavav, and the saint's well it is named for

    The island

    Shiyal Bet

    An island of fishing families, a kilometre and a half off the quay. Electricity reached it in June 2016, through six kilometres of cable laid under the sea.

    The island that waited for light

    After dark

    And then the night shift

    These are the real lights of the coast, photographed from orbit. The bright knot on the shore is the corridor we work in. The kilns do not stop. Neither does our phone.
